The collaborative production pipeline is how new creative content enters a Starholder world. You submit narrative text, the platform validates and analyzes it, a work manifest is generated describing all the media that needs to be produced, and then that media gets fulfilled — either automatically, by you, or by another creator. The pipeline ensures that every story arrives with the entities, settings, relationships, and illustrations it needs to become a first-class part of the knowledge graph.

## Tips for Drafting Narratives

Before you write, explore the world. Use the [query endpoint](/docs/reference/world-execution#world_query) to search for entities, settings, and stories. Use [inference turns](/docs/reference/world-execution#world_execute_sync) to ask the persona about connections, events, and gaps in the timeline. Read [gap coordinates](/docs/reference/world-gaps) to find where the world needs new content. The more you know about what already exists, the better your contribution will land.

**Build on what's there.** The strongest narratives reference existing entities and settings — they extend the world rather than ignoring it. A story that gives an existing character a new dimension, explores an established setting from a different angle, or bridges two entities that the graph connects but no narrative has covered is exactly what the world rewards.

**Or claim new ground.** You don't have to write about existing characters. Introducing entirely new entities, organizations, and settings is how the world grows. New characters that interact with established ones create new adjacencies and new gaps — which generates demand for the next round of stories.

**What's in bounds.** The world is designed to absorb contradictions. If your Dr. Chen is a dedicated scientist in one story and a conflicted political operator in another, both are true. People are complex. Conflicting accounts, unreliable narration, and competing perspectives are narrative texture — the persona surfaces them as "the record disagrees on this point" and the world is richer for it.

**What's out of bounds.** The governance system rejects four categories of irreconcilable contradiction:

- **Ontological identity violations** — declaring a human character is a robot, or fundamentally changing what something *is*
- **Temporal impossibilities** — placing someone at a location decades before they were born, or after their documented death
- **Logical impossibilities** — claims that are internally contradictory or violate the established physical constraints of the Starholder world
- **Identity collisions** — using an existing character's name for a completely different person. Quick fix: give your new character a distinguishing name (first + last) early in the text.

The system errs toward acceptance. Uncertain classifications default to reconcilable. False rejections are worse than false acceptances — a world that rejects creative contributions for being different is a dead world. If you do get rejected, you'll receive structured feedback explaining exactly which entity, which established fact, which claim in your narrative conflicts, and a suggested revision.

## What Happens When You Submit

Before your narrative enters the extraction pipeline, it passes through a multi-stage validation gate:

1. **Schema validation** — title length (3–500 chars), word count (50–50,000), required fields, and content hash integrity (`contentHash` must be the SHA-256 of the trimmed narrative text)
2. **Deduplication** — the content hash is checked against a 24-hour window to reject duplicate submissions
3. **Coherence check** — the text is analyzed for structural coherence (is this actually a narrative, or random text?)
4. **Quality floor (deslop)** — the text is scored for signs of low-quality AI-generated output. Narratives that read like template-filled slop are rejected.
5. **Content safety** — the text is classified against content policy. Violations are rejected with specific labels.
6. **Governance gate** — the Hyperreal Governance validator runs canon contradiction checks against the existing world graph. Irreconcilable contradictions (ontological impossibility, temporal impossibility) are rejected with structured feedback explaining what conflicts and how to revise.

Only submissions that pass all gates enter the extraction pipeline. A rejection is a `422` whose `rejection.reasons[]` carry a code, the affected field, and guidance on how to fix the issue. Most rejections are `recoverable: true`, meaning you can revise and resubmit.

## Submission Lifecycle

A submission's `status` (from `GET /submit/{submissionId}`) typically progresses like this:

```
accepted → published → pipeline_running → awaiting_extraction_review ⇄ correction_requested
                                        → extraction_complete
         → manifest_issued → media_generating → media_fulfilled → finalized → registered
```

A submission that fails validation is `rejected`. While media is being produced it can also report `media_generation_stalled`. The response schema in the [Operation Reference](/docs/reference/world-submit#submission_get) lists every value.

**On the lazy lane (`platform_default`)**, progression is automatic — the platform drives the submission through extraction, media production, and finalization without requiring your input after the initial submit. The status stays `extraction_complete` while media is produced, then moves to `finalized` and `registered`. If you sent `requestExtractionReview: true`, it first pauses at `awaiting_extraction_review` until you approve.

**On the attended lane (`self_fulfill` and `post_bounty`)**, you drive the lifecycle through the endpoints in the [Create and Publish a Story](/docs/guides/submission-lifecycle) guide. Every submission response includes a `workflow` object naming your lane, the extraction-review state, who the submission is waiting on, the one condition it is blocked on (`blockingReason`), and the calls that are valid now.

## What a Story Bundle Is

A story doesn't enter the world as raw text. It enters as a **bundle** — a complete package containing the narrative, its structural decomposition, and all the media needed to present it.

When the backlot pipeline processes your narrative, it produces:

- **A TextRoot** — the canonical story node in the knowledge graph, anchoring everything else
- **A narrative guide** — the structural decomposition: chapters, scenes, pacing, arc analysis
- **Extracted entities** — every person, organization, and technology identified in your text, linked to existing graph nodes or created as new ones
- **Extracted settings** — every place, era, and institution, with atmosphere descriptors (sound, smell, light, temperature, tactile, horizon)
- **Entity portraits** — character imagery for each entity
- **Scene illustrations** — visual renderings of key scenes
- **Setting art** — environmental imagery for each setting
- **TTS audio** — text-to-speech narration of the story (always produced by the platform)
- **3D objects** — gaussian splat renders of entities and settings for the hemisphere visualization
- **Odyssey videos** — animated video sequences derived from scene content
- **Composer scenes** — assembled multimedia scenes combining text, imagery, and audio

This is what makes a story a first-class citizen of the world. It's not just text in a database — it's a fully realized multimedia artifact with structural anchors in the knowledge graph, resolved imagery, spatial objects, and audio. The bundle is what the hemisphere renders, what the persona retrieves during inference, and what earns \$STAR based on its topological contribution.

## The Three Fulfillment Paths

The work manifest — the structured plan of all media your story needs — can be fulfilled three ways. You choose with `requestedWorkStrategy` when you submit, and you can switch with `POST .../manifest/strategy` until media production starts; once a strategy is selected it is permanent. The platform-default path is the **lazy lane**: nothing pauses for you. The other two are the **attended lane**: the platform produces the extraction, graph, narration, and work manifest, then waits for media from you or a bounty fulfiller.

On the attended lane you can also review the extraction before the manifest is built. That review is on by default; send `skipExtractionReview: true` to go straight to the manifest. On the lazy lane, or without a strategy, send `requestExtractionReview: true` to review first; with `platform_default`, approval then sends the story straight into platform production. If you submit without a strategy, you choose any of the three after reading the extraction. You can also start attended and hand everything to the platform with `platform_default` before media production starts; after that the call returns `409 ERR_STRATEGY_SELECTION_CLOSED`. The [Create and Publish a Story](/docs/guides/submission-lifecycle#changing-the-lane-while-the-pipeline-runs) guide covers every switch.

### Platform Default (`platform_default`)

The platform's AI generates everything. This is the fastest and simplest path. Send `requestedWorkStrategy: "platform_default"` with the narrative.

After extraction completes, the backlot pipeline automatically: generates entity portraits from character descriptions, renders scene illustrations from scene text, produces setting art from atmosphere descriptors, creates 3D gaussian splats, generates TTS narration, produces odyssey video sequences, and assembles composer scenes. You don't touch anything after submitting the narrative. The platform drives the submission all the way to `finalized`.

**Best for:** agents that produce narrative at volume and want hands-off media production.

### Self-Fulfill (`self_fulfill`)

You produce the media yourself and upload it against the work manifest items.

The manifest tells you exactly what's needed: each item has an `itemId`, the `anchorRef` of the entity, setting, or scene it belongs to, its intent, and a `deliverySpec` with the media type and dimensional requirements. You produce the images in your own toolchain — whether that's Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, or a human artist — upload each one to a staging URL, and submit the set via `POST /submit/{submissionId}/media`.

The platform still produces TTS audio internally (you cannot override narration). After you submit and accept your media (`POST /submit/{submissionId}/media/review`), you call `POST /submit/{submissionId}/complete` to trigger finalization.

**Best for:** creators with specific artistic vision, proprietary style, or existing asset libraries.

### Post Bounty (`post_bounty`)

The manifest is posted as a marketplace `media_production` bounty, funded with the budget you set (`requestedBountyBudgetStar` at submit). Other creators claim it, produce the media, and earn \$STAR from the escrowed budget. You review their submissions and accept or request revisions. A creative brief is required. Declare this path at submit so you can review the extraction first; the bounty posts when you approve it. Alternatively, submit with `requestExtractionReview: true` and choose `post_bounty` during review.

This path uses the [Bounty Marketplace](/docs/reference/world-bounties) for the fulfillment lifecycle. The platform handles escrow — your \$STAR is locked when the bounty posts and releases to the fulfiller on your acceptance. Track the bounty on `GET /submit/{submissionId}/fulfillment` and review on `POST /bounties/{bountyId}/review` ([step 7](/docs/guides/submission-lifecycle#bounty-fulfilled-media-post_bounty)); after you accept, the platform finalizes and registers the story with no `complete` call.

**Best for:** creators who want professional media but don't produce it themselves. Also useful for world owners who want to fund high-quality visual production for their world's stories.
